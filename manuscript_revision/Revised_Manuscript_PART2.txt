
severe-impact range (>=60) to approximately the little-to-no-impact border in the
supraorbital arm (49.48) and to "some impact" in the infraorbital arm (51.40) [32]. Oral
analgesic frequency decreased by 65.7% and 61.6%, respectively, and MPQ-5 scores also
declined with active treatment. Medication-related benefits are clinically relevant given
the risks of adverse effects and medication-overuse headache with pharmacological
management [10,34,35], although pooled evidence for related non-invasive modalities
remains mixed for some medication-day endpoints [40].


4.5. Clinical implications

Pending confirmatory research, either stimulation site may be considered when TENS is used
preventively in patients able to attend an intensive in-clinic schedule; practical factors
(montage feasibility, tolerability, preference) may guide site choice more than a claimed
efficacy advantage. Expert consensus supports neuromodulation for selected patients,
particularly those refractory to or intolerant of drugs [36,37]. The present protocol does
not establish acute abortive efficacy. Home-based, fewer-session, or remotely supervised
regimens will be needed before broader implementation can be recommended.


4.6. Strengths

Strengths include randomized sham control, investigator-blinded outcome assessment and
analysis, multidimensional migraine-specific outcomes, balanced baseline demographics for
age and sex, and 3-month follow-up. To our knowledge, this is among the first RCTs to
compare transcutaneous supraorbital and infraorbital stimulation directly in migraine.


4.7. Limitations and future directions

Several limitations constrain interpretation and generalizability:


1. Pre-randomization selection bias: 54/137 screened patients were excluded solely for inability to commit to 24 sessions. The analyzed sample is enriched for high adherence and does not represent the broader migraine population. We deliberately avoided imputing never-randomized individuals into a single study arm, as that approach is statistically invalid; selection bias is therefore acknowledged rather than "adjusted away."
============================================================


2. Underpowered site comparison: Achieved power for the planned 3-attack contrast was approx. 64%; the non-significant supraorbital-versus-infraorbital result is inconclusive, not evidence of equivalence.
============================================================


3. Sham-site asymmetry: Sham replicated only the supraorbital montage; the infraorbital-versus-sham contrast is not site-matched and may partly reflect montage-specific expectancy or sensory cues.
============================================================


4. Incomplete participant blinding with exclusively patient-reported outcomes raises the possibility that expectation inflated effect estimates.
============================================================


5. Approximate Welch CIs accompany non-parametric tests and are descriptive rather than model-consistent interval estimates.
============================================================


6. Follow-up of 3 months does not establish durability.
=======================================================


7. Adverse events were captured by open-ended inquiry rather than a standardized checklist.
============================================================


8. Single-center design and a predominantly female sample (75.9%) limit external validity.
============================================================


9. No pre-specified subgroup analyses by migraine subtype (episodic vs chronic; with vs without aura), despite high baseline attack frequency suggestive of a high-frequency cohort.
============================================================


10. Ethics approval and IRCT numbers remain placeholders and must be inserted before journal submission.
============================================================

Future trials should use larger samples; site-matched, more credible sham conditions;
rigorous blinding with pre-specified blinding indices; longer follow-up; a locked
statistical analysis plan; mechanistic imaging or biomarkers; dose-finding designs; and
predictors of response. Pragmatic evaluations of lower-burden delivery models are also
needed.


5. Conclusions
==============

In a selected, highly adherent outpatient population, preventive TENS over either the
supraorbital or infraorbital trigeminal branch improved monthly migraine attack frequency
and secondary patient-reported outcomes relative to sham at 3 months. No statistically
significant between-site difference was demonstrated; given limited power and sham-site
asymmetry, this finding is inconclusive rather than evidence of equivalence. Descriptive
early differences in VAS trajectory are hypothesis-generating only. Confirmation in
larger, site-matched, more rigorously blinded trials with longer follow-up and verifiable
prospective registration is required before strong practice recommendations can be made.
