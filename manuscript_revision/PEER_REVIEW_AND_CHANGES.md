# Q1-Style Peer Review Report & Revision Summary

**Manuscript:** Comparative RCT of supraorbital vs infraorbital TENS for migraine prevention  
**Reviewer stance:** Major revision required for Q1 neurology / headache journals (e.g., *Cephalalgia*, *J Headache Pain*, *Headache*, *Neurology* clinical trials bar)  
**Deliverable:** `Migraine_TENS_RCT_Revised_Q1.docx`

---

## Overall verdict

**Major revision.** The clinical question is relevant and the sham-controlled, three-arm design is a strength. However, several methodological and reporting issues would block acceptance at a high-impact Q1 journal in the current form. The revised file corrects scientific overstatement, removes an invalid sensitivity analysis, tightens structure, and aligns claims with what the data can support.

---

## Major concerns (addressed in the revised manuscript)

### 1. Invalid “worst-case” sensitivity analysis (critical)
Imputing 54 **pre-randomization** exclusions into the **sham arm only** is not a valid sensitivity analysis. Those individuals were never randomized and cannot be assigned to a post-allocation group. Claiming that this “confirms robustness” would be rejected by a statistical reviewer.

**Revision:** Removed Tables 8 and related Abstract/Results claims. Selection bias is now acknowledged qualitatively in Methods and Limitations.

### 2. Overstatement of between-site conclusions
A non-significant supraorbital–infraorbital contrast under ~64% power is **inconclusive**, not equivalence.

**Revision:** Title, Abstract, Discussion, and Conclusions consistently state this. Removed Table 9 (descriptive “interpretation” table that invited over-reading).

### 3. Title inflation
“Highly adherent population” belongs in Methods/Limitations, not as a title framing device that can read as marketing.

**Revision:** Title shortened to a standard RCT formulation; adherence enrichment retained in text.

### 4. Sham-site asymmetry under-emphasized relative to claims
Infraorbital-vs-sham is not site-matched.

**Revision:** Flagged in Methods, Discussion, Abstract conclusions, and as a numbered limitation.

### 5. Incomplete blinding + PRO-only endpoints
Expectation bias is a central threat to validity.

**Revision:** Effect sizes are framed as potentially inflated; language softened from confirmatory to preliminary.

### 6. Ethics / IRCT placeholders
Empty registration fields are a submission blocker.

**Revision:** Marked explicitly as `[TO BE COMPLETED]` in Abstract and Declarations (authors must insert real codes).

### 7. Structural / numbering errors
Original had duplicated “4.7” logic (strengths vs limitations), redundant repetition of pulse-width/device sentences, and a contradictory Bonferroni narrative (“no correction” vs “Bonferroni applied”).

**Revision:** Clean section numbering; single coherent multiplicity statement (Bonferroni α = 0.01 for secondary outcomes).

## Suggested decision letter tone (for authors’ internal use)

> The study addresses an interesting comparative neuromodulation question. Active TENS appears superior to an inert supraorbital sham in a selected adherent sample. However, the between-site comparison is underpowered, the sham is site-asymmetric, blinding is incomplete for patient-reported outcomes, and the previous sensitivity analysis of pre-randomization exclusions was invalid. After correcting these issues, the manuscript is more suitable as a **preliminary / hypothesis-generating** RCT report rather than a confirmatory comparative-effectiveness claim.
